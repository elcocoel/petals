# Vesting

A token balance does not say when someone can use it. Find each allocation's lock, read its schedule from the chain where the chain has it, write it as `vesting/v1`, and Petals cuts the petals that hold it.

Petals may already hold a token's vesting: `get_map`'s `vesting` gives it now, each allocation's unlocked, next unlock and later and their totals, by its schedules. Answer from it first, say when it was read and how each allocation is known; read anew only where it's missing or old.

1. **Find the lock.** On a holders map, look at the largest holders that aren't people: a balance owned by a lock program is an allocation's lock. Ask the chain who owns the account: Magna, Streamflow, Jupiter Lock, Sablier, Hedgey, an OpenZeppelin [VestingWallet](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/api/finance), a [NEAR lockup](https://github.com/near/core-contracts/blob/master/lockup/README.md). A project's published allocation table says what to look for; it is a lead, not proof that the tokens sit there.
2. **Read what the chain gives.** A stream keeps its terms in its own account ([Streamflow's SDK](https://github.com/streamflow-finance/js-sdk/blob/master/packages/stream/README.md) reads them); a VestingWallet has its start and duration; a NEAR lockup its lockup timestamp, release duration and vesting schedule. A Merkle distributor (Magna) holds one root on chain, and each schedule is a leaf seen only inside a Withdraw with its proof: find the claim records the program owns, then each record's first Withdraw, and read the leaf's pieces (start, period, count, amount). Record each release as the chain says it, with the transaction.
3. **Fill the gaps as leads and inferences.** An allocation that never withdrew has no leaf on chain. Take its share and lock-up from the project's table (published) and its dates and steps from a leaf with the same lock-up (inferred), and say which. Cross-check with DefiLlama's emissions dataset (`defillama-datasets.llama.fi/emissions/<slug>`): its cliffs and its total unlocked.
4. **Keep four answers separate.** Vested is earned under the schedule; unlocked is past its restriction; claimable is available to withdraw now; transferable may mean the tokens or the right to claim them. Unlocked by the schedule (computed) and withdrawn (on chain) are two numbers. Note who can cancel, revoke, amend or move the claim, and any condition beyond time. Voting rights can exist while tokens stay locked: follow the governance contract.
5. **Write it as `vesting/v1`.** One allocation each: `name`; `holder`, the account its locked tokens sit in (CAIP-10); `beneficiary`, who can claim them, or null; `total` in whole tokens; `schedule`, lumps `{at, amount}` and streams `{from, to, every, amount}` (`every` in seconds, the first step at from + every and the last at `to`; null for continuous), adding up to the total; `released` `{amount, at}`, or null; `known` (`verified`, `published` or `inferred`); `how`, in a sentence; and `evidence`. The document names its map (`about`), its token with its decimals, and `read_at`. Keep it in the person's project with `keep_data`; Petals' own mapping loads it onto the shared map.
6. **How it cuts the petals.** At a reading's date, the petal of each holder account is cut bottom to top: `unlocked` (what it holds beyond what its schedules still lock), `next unlock` (what unlocks at the next moment one does) and `later` (the rest still locked), in the reading's unit, adding up to what it holds. It also gets `next_unlock`, a date Colour and Order can take, and `tranche`, the allocation's name, where it holds one. Choose the holder so the cut adds up: a distributor holding six allocations is one petal cut by all six; a stream per allocation gives each its own petal and tranche. Where the schedules lock more than the petal holds, they don't describe it, and it isn't cut: check the holder and the totals.

The worked example is TRUMP: 71.8% of its supply sits with Magna's Merkle distributor (`2RH6rUTPBJ9rUDPpuV9b8z1YL56k1tYU6Uk5ZoaEFFSK`). Four of the six "Creators & CIC Digital" allocations withdrew, so their leaves are on chain (a 10% or 25% cliff, then 730 daily steps at 09:00 UTC); CIC 1 and 2 never did, so their terms are the published table's, with the dates of the leaf with the same lock-up. `read_chain` reads EVM contracts, not NEAR or Solana: on those chains, read with the public RPC from the person's machine (Solana's `getProgramAccounts`, `getSignaturesForAddress`, `getTransaction`) and write the document there. Call a schedule verified only with its transaction or account; label deductions inferred and tables leads; unknown stays unknown. A finding about one allocation can also go in the project as an annotation (`add_annotation`, a `noted` claim with its contract, block or slot and source as evidence).
