Explore what others have mapped, and add the readings you care about. All data sets come together in the Power Map. It gets more detailed as we explore.

## Start a conversation

1. [Connect your wallet](/docs/use-it/connect-your-wallet) and sign in.
2. Open [/make](/make) and type a community, a wallet or a contract to look into, such as “Who holds power in RaidGuild?” or a NEAR account’s name.
3. Petals’ agent finds it, reads the chain and draws its flower beside the chat.

Good next questions: “Show me the source.” “Who’s behind this petal?” “How did this change over the last year?”

You don’t have to know the whole investigation before you begin. Ask the next question, and keep mapping.

## Five questions about power

The brain guides the agent through five questions, each read from the chain:

- **Who holds?** A token’s holders, a DAO’s shares or loot, a validator’s stake.
- **Who votes?** Votes cast, delegated voting power, who sponsored proposals.
- **Who can move the money?** A treasury’s signers, the owners of a multisig.
- **Who can change the rules?** An owner, an admin key, who can upgrade or pause a contract.
- **How do you join or leave?** A token you buy, a vote to admit, a right to quit with your part.

Each can draw a different flower for the same DAO. [How to read a flower](/docs/ideas/read-a-flower) says what to check in each.

## A system Petals hasn’t read

Today’s readers cover specific systems on NEAR, Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Gnosis and Solana. New systems may need new recipes or additional tools, and every reading will make its limits clear.

When no recipe knows a contract, the agent works it out and says so as it goes: it reads what the contract answers, finds where power is, reads a small range first and checks it against what the contract itself says, and keeps what it read as a layer on your flower. If the system is worth reading again, it can write a recipe and offer it as a pull request, so every agent can read it next time. See [Teach Petals a new system](/docs/build-it-with-us/contributing).

## Over time

Each reading captures a moment, identified by its date or block. Ask for a fresh reading, or use time to frame your investigation: “Show this network before and after that proposal,” or “How did voting power change each month?”

Where historical data is available, the agent can build a sequence of snapshots and compare who gained power, who lost it and how connections changed. Each snapshot preserves the data for its moment. Your current visual settings determine how every snapshot is drawn; changing them changes the view across the comparison.
