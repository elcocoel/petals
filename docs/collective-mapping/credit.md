Credit is kept per fact, for every author who stated it, in the order they published: the first, then each who matched it. Nothing takes credit away: a later publication never removes an earlier one's.

## Your credit record

Each author has a credit record, worked out from the publications alone, so anyone can work it out again and get the same answer. For each layer you published, it holds:

- when you first published it;
- the facts you stated **first**, and the facts you **matched**, stated before you by someone else;
- who **built on** it: other authors whose published layers name it among what they were built on;
- who **reused** it: other authors whose published versions include it, or fork a version that does.

Another account counts once per layer, however many times it used it. Your own use never counts.

## Points

A layer earns one point for each other account that built on it or reused it.

- **Building on follows the chain back.** A layer built on a layer that was built on yours counts for yours too, once.
- **Only what's published counts,** and only other authors' published versions count as use.
- **Copying earns nothing.** A use gives its point only to the authors who had published that layer before the use. Publishing an already-used layer again earns you a match, an independent confirmation, never points from earlier uses.
- **Stating facts first or matching them is credit, not points.**

Your points are the sum over your layers. They aren't money, and there's no token. Points are proof of work for the common good: a future airdrop or governance token, even one without economic value, could read them. The record keeps who used what, so anything built later can read it.

## The hard cases

They've been considered. Here is how each is handled now, or will be.

- **Two authors, two values for the same slot.** Two reads of the same chain data at the same block should give the same value, so a difference is near impossible for chain data, and when it happens it's flagged for anyone to check. Both values stand, each credited to its own authors. Today the value published first is shown, with the others beside it as options. Next, the options are ordered by a qualifier's verdict and each author's standing.
- **Copying.** It earns nothing. A use gives its point only to the authors who had published that layer before the use, so publishing someone's layer again earns a match, never their points.
- **Many wallets, one person.** Several wallets can be one person, and accounts acting together can reuse each other. Each account counts once per layer, your own use never counts, and before any reward someone must confirm usefulness and attribution, with that decision itself recorded.
- **Claims.** A claim is what someone says, not a chain read, so claims can differ honestly. All of them are shown, each with who said it and its evidence. A fuller treatment comes later.

## What credit doesn't say

- **That work was useful.** No count here proves it.
- **Who someone is.** A wallet isn't a person. An agent's name is what its author called it, not a proven identity.
- **That a fact is right.** A match is two reads agreeing. A fact that differs is a question for anyone to check, not a verdict on who was wrong.
- **That a fact was new to the world.** Credit says who published a fact on Petals first, and who matched it.
- **That a reading is complete.** A reading covers what it states. A period read doesn't prove nothing else happened in it.
- **That older data is anyone's.** What Petals loaded before the record began is one import by `mapping.petals.near`, unsigned: a collective label, not a person's credit.
