**Power has a shape. Now you can see it.** Point Petals at any wallet or contract, or just name a community, and map how power moves.

## The story

### Tell Petals what to focus on.

Name a community, wallet or contract. Petals’ agent, powered by NEAR AI, explores it with you. A flower shows how power is held and how accounts connect.

### Connect your own agents.

Petals can outsource the heavy mapping and research to agents you pay for. They work with their own tools and resources, bringing results back while you keep exploring with Petals.

### Behind Petals is a Brain

Your swarm can coordinate seamlessly as you chat because of the Brain. An open library of methods that guides agents on how to query and analyze power networks.

### Deterministic results

Tools read the chain. Each reading records its sources and date, with interpretations marked separately. The flower makes the results visible; the agent helps you make sense of them.

### Each ask grows the map

Explore what others have mapped, and add the readings you care about. All data sets come together in the Power Map. It gets more detailed as we explore.

### Pick up where you left off.

Connect a NEAR wallet to save your conversations, notes and maps. Return to them and keep working until you are ready to publish. Your workspace stays private and your contributions use your NEAR Social profile.

### A few cents go a long way.

Browsing the Power Map is free. Top up your Petals account to use its agent and mapping tools, paying as you go. Connected agents use their own resources for the work they take on.

### What can I do with what I find?

Keep notes alongside the evidence, return to an investigation, and compare networks or different moments in their history. Use what you learn to prepare a proposal, understand who might support it, choose a delegate or question a decision. You can also ask the agent to help turn an investigation into a clear explanation for your community.

A useful finding can be a concentration of power, a connection worth investigating or something the available records cannot explain. When you share it, include the sources, date and open questions so others can check it and continue the work.

## What a flower shows

**Accounts become petals. Together they shape a network.**

The shape of a “flower” gives you a first look at power: whether it’s spread across a community or concentrated in a few accounts, and how those accounts connect. What counts as power depends on what you’re exploring. Petals’ Brain guides the agent in helping you interpret the data.

Each petal represents an account. A large one might represent many others through a staking pool, an exchange or a shared treasury. One person can also control several accounts. Petals can help you find clues.

A flower is drawn from its data and a view: settings that assign pieces of data to visual dimensions such as height, width, colour and segments. Height might show a share of voting power; bands inside a petal might show the backers behind a staking pool. Changing the view changes how you read the data, not the underlying records.
