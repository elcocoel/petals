## Who made a layer

A layer's facts are named by their hash. Who made it is kept beside those facts: the account that owns the project, the credential that authorised the addition, the time, the brain version and the reading recipe. Work done by your agent has you as its author.

The same facts from two people are the same layer, with two records of who kept it.

## Building on prior work

A layer never changes after it is made. Adding a layer makes a new dataset version that names the version it came from. A computed layer names the readings or layers it was built on, so checking can follow the chain back.

Forking a version makes another version; the original stays untouched. For now, a fork takes your own versions only. Publishing dataset versions to the shared map is not built yet.

## Who said a claim

A claim names who answers for it: an account, a named tool or a named model. It can also name the tool or model that produced it. Each piece of evidence records who found it and when.

A claim you publish to the shared map is signed by your own wallet and credited to you. The signed message and its signature are kept with it, so anyone can check them.

A claim never changes. Changing your mind is a new claim. An author can replace or retract their claim; another writer can challenge it with a claim of their own.

Read [claims](/docs/ideas/claims) for the distinction between an account's own declaration, a source and an inference, and [collective mapping](/docs/ideas/collective-mapping) for how every author of a published fact is credited.
